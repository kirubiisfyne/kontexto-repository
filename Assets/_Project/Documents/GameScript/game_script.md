### Document Task

> [!info] Formatting Skill - Introduce: Font Face & Size
> Welcome to your first lesson in document formatting. In professional and academic writing, the visual presentation of your text is just as important as the content itself. 
> 
> * **Font Face** refers to the text's design. In academic writing, use traditional Serif fonts (e.g., *Times New Roman*) for printed essays, or clean Sans-serif fonts (e.g., *Arial*) for digital viewing. Always avoid informal novelty fonts.
> * **Font Size** establishes a clear visual hierarchy using points (pt). Standardize your main body text at 11pt or 12pt, use 14pt to 16pt for subheadings, and reserve 18pt or higher strictly for main titles.

- [ ] Create an Official Staff Profile. The "Messy" State starts with "Malaya/Francisco Magbanua" in tiny Comic Sans. Change the Font Face to a professional standard and increase the Font Size.
- [ ] **Failure Condition (The "Red Pen" Feedback):** "Incorrect, Try Again."

> [!info] Formatting Skill - Font Face & Size, Introduce: Text Emphasis (Bold / Italics)
> Text Emphasis is used to highlight specific words and guide the reader's attention. 
> 
> * **Bold** text appears thicker and darker; it should be used strategically to draw the eye to crucial vocabulary, key concepts, or structural elements like headings. 
> * *Italicized* text slants to the right and provides a softer emphasis. In academic writing, italics are strictly reserved for the titles of major works (such as books or journals), foreign terms, or to gently stress a specific word within a sentence.

> 
> * **First Line Indent:** Shifts only the first sentence of a paragraph to the right (standard for academic paragraph transitions).
> * **Hanging Indent:** Keeps the first line flush left while indenting every subsequent line (mandatory for bibliographies, reference lists, and structured citations).
